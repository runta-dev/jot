# R15.2 fixed-candidate results

26 prefix states × 2 orderings × 4 conditions. These are correlated conditional decisions from four previously generated replies, not independent chat tasks.

|Condition|Identical selections across orderings|
|---|---:|
|full_base|19/26|
|full_grounded|17/26|
|fragment_base|18/26|
|fragment_grounded|17/26|

## Critical capability state

At `I can help with`, full/base selects “anything” in original order and “programming” in reverse. Full/grounded selects “programming” in both. Fragment/base and fragment/grounded both select “various” in both orders. Thus grounding avoids the universal claim on this one state; it does not prove programming capability or that the resulting answer will be useful. Candidate order materially changes the ungrounded choice.

## Interpretation

This experiment cannot establish that full continuations globally outperform fragments, because candidates and prefixes were generated by the full method. Conditional stability is not correctness. A fresh end-to-end matched ablation is needed. Avoid concluding that generic grounding has fixed hallucinations from a single state.

Next: end-to-end full versus fragment, both grounded, on the same two development conversations with exact actual generated history. Keep vocabulary, budgets, order, and stopping rules identical; deduplicate rendered alternatives in both. Only the full continuation representation changes. No production integration.
